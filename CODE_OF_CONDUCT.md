# Code of Conduct

## Simple is safe ground.

OpenSumai (住まい) means *home / dwelling* — a tool that makes buying a house in Japan
easy and trackable. This community should work the same way: somewhere anyone, from a
first-time homebuyer to a seasoned engineer, can stand without getting burned. The rules
below aren't legal boilerplate; they're what "safe ground" looks like when we build
together.

## Who this belongs to

Everyone taking part in an OpenSumai project — issues, pull requests, discussions,
reviews, email — whether you've shipped a hundred apps or you're filing your first bug
report. You don't have to be technical to belong here. We explain things, we don't
gatekeep, and we assume good faith.

## What keeps the floor safe

- Be kind, and assume the person on the other end is doing their best.
- Welcome questions — including basic ones. Meet people where they are.
- Critique the work, not the person, and take that kind of feedback in return.
- **Respect privacy the way the product does.** OpenSumai is private-by-default and
  BYOK; hold each other to that too. Don't share anyone's listings, addresses, budget
  signals, or keys without their clear permission.
- Own your mistakes, fix them, and move on.

## What gets you burned

- Harassment, intimidation, or personal attacks — public or private.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, or deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission ("doxxing").
- Anything a reasonable person would find out of place in a friendly, professional project.

## Where this applies

Every OpenSumai space — our repositories, issue trackers, and any channel where you're
taking part — and any time you're representing the project in public.

## If something goes wrong

OpenSumai is a small, mostly one-person project, so here's the honest version: if
something crosses a line, report it via **GitHub — Report a vulnerability** on this
repo's **Security ▸ Advisories** tab (a dedicated conduct mailbox is not yet available).
Tell me what happened, where, and when — links or screenshots help.

I'll read every report, look into it fairly, and keep your identity and the details
private — the same respect for privacy the product is built on. Reporting something in
good faith will never be held against you.

## What happens next

Depending on what happened, the response ranges from a quiet word to a permanent ban:

1. **A quiet correction.** For a one-off — a private note explaining what was off and why.
2. **A warning.** For a clearer or repeated problem: a warning plus a period of no
   interaction with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban.** For a serious violation or sustained bad behavior.
4. **A permanent ban.** For a pattern of violations, harassment, or contempt toward a
   group of people.
